# ceo-alignment

Draft a CEO and CTO operating agreement. Use `references/ceo-cto-operating-agreement.md` as the template and `references/ceo-cto-partnership.md` for the practices behind it. Both are bundled with this skill.

## Step 1: Interview

Ask in one message. Accept partial answers.

1. Company stage, size and the CEO's name and background (technical or not).
2. How long you have worked with the CEO, and how the relationship is going today.
3. The CEO's top three outcomes for the next twelve months, in the CEO's words if known.
4. Decisions that are already contested or unclear (hiring, budget, architecture, roadmap, vendors, customer commitments).
5. Current meeting rhythm with the CEO and the board calendar.
6. Resource constraints: budget, headcount, runway, hiring freeze.
7. Past surprises: something the CEO learned from someone else first.
8. What you want from the CEO that you are not getting, and what you think the CEO wants from you.

## Step 2: Draft

Fill every section of the template from the interview. For each decision-rights row, propose a D, R, C or I and a limit, and give one line of reasoning. Mark each proposal **Proposed**. Do not present proposals as agreed.

Where the user gave no information, leave the cell empty and add it to a list of questions. Do not invent numbers, limits or names.

## Step 3: Produce

Output in this order:

1. **The agreement**, using the template headings.
2. **Questions to settle with the CEO**: the cells you could not fill, ranked by how much they affect the relationship.
3. **Three points most likely to cause friction**, each with a suggested wording for raising it.
4. **A 20-minute meeting plan** for walking the CEO through it.

## Rules

- Plain, direct language. No filler and no hedging.
- Write as a working agreement, not a legal document. Remind the user that compensation, equity and termination belong in the offer letter and equity documents, and to ask a lawyer about those.
- Never claim the CEO has agreed to anything.
- Keep the bad-news timing and the no-surprises rule in every draft.
- Offer to revisit the agreement at 30 and 90 days.
